\begin{center}
    \textbf{\large ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\vspace{14pt}

\begin{singlespace}
\noindent\justifying
Information about lost animals is usually spread across social media posts, messaging groups and printed posters, which makes it difficult to gather information and follow up on a search. This work presents WeFIND, a collaborative mobile application that centralizes the dissemination of lost and found animals and organizes community participation based on the location of each case. The applied research combined a questionnaire with pet owners and animal protectors and a comparative analysis of similar platforms, which identified the loss of reach of posts, the lack of maps and the exposure of personal contact details as the main difficulties. Based on these findings, the system's requirements and UML models were defined, and the application was developed iteratively and incrementally with React Native and Expo on the mobile side and the Supabase platform, backed by a PostgreSQL database, for authentication, storage and real-time synchronization. The application allows users to publish and browse reports in a list and on a map, filter them by proximity and animal characteristics, register sightings linked to each search, chat privately and generate QR-code posters. As a distinguishing feature, WeFIND includes a heuristic matching mechanism that compares lost and found reports based on species, physical characteristics and geographic distance, calculated with the Haversine formula, and suggests possible reunions for users to confirm. In a test with 20 fictitious pairs of reports, the mechanism suggested eight of the ten pairs describing the same animal, but also six of the ten pairs describing different animals, which reinforces the need for confirmation by people. The usability evaluation with pet owners, based on the System Usability Scale (SUS), indicated a high level of satisfaction with ease of use and pointed out improvements for the interface. It is concluded that WeFIND brings together, in a single tool, features that were not found in an integrated way in the analyzed platforms, helping to make the search for lost animals more organized and collaborative.

\vspace{18pt}

\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} Lost animals. Mobile applications. Geolocation. Collaborative systems. Heuristic matching.
\end{singlespace}
